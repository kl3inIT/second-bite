# Second Bite — project introduction

*A second chance for good food.*

## What I am building

I am developing Second Bite, a bilingual food-surplus marketplace for Vietnam. The goal is to help nearby customers buy affordable food while businesses recover value from genuine edible surplus. I want to test whether a simple local transaction can create both commercial value and measurable waste reduction.

## How it would work

Businesses confirm suitable excess stock and offer discounted boxes of ingredients, bakery products or prepared food when available. Ingredients still needed for normal later service stay in the kitchen. Customers see the offer, price and collection window, reserve confirmed stock and collect directly from the seller using a one-use code.

The design addresses two practical obstacles: uncertain supply and inconvenient pickup times. Expected surplus can be followed without purchase; confirmed stock can be reserved. Collection planning, reminders and collection by another person help customers complete the handover. Staff must still check food suitability and handling.

## How I use AI

I used AI coding assistance to turn my ideas into an interactive prototype and refine the customer, merchant and administrator journeys. This helped me build and explore the workflow despite having no formal computer science background. I remain responsible for product decisions and for checking the output.

The current in-app listing and matching tools use explicit rules, rather than a connected AI model. My proposed next AI experiment is turning a seller's short description into a structured listing draft for staff to review. I would measure time saved and corrections required. AI would not decide whether food is safe.

## Current stage and next step

The prototype demonstrates bilingual browsing, ingredient offers, reservations, simulated payments and pickup controls. Grouped boxes are the next product iteration. There are no verified live customers or revenue yet. I plan to observe local sellers, establish genuine surplus availability and prepare the engineering, food-safety and legal requirements before a small Hanoi pilot.

Success would mean reliable paid collections, repeat customers, active sellers and workable economics. Avoided disposal would be measured separately against a baseline.
